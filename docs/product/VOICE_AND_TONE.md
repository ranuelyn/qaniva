# Qaniva voice & tone

Qaniva speaks like a calm senior clinician doing a good debrief: clear,
specific, respectful of the learner, and honest about uncertainty.

**Principles:** clear · calm · clinical · concise · non-patronizing ·
educational · confident without overstating certainty.

| Instead of | Qaniva says |
| --- | --- |
| "Awesome!" / "Perfect!" | "Case complete" |
| "You failed!" | "The patient deteriorated before definitive care" |
| "You saved the patient!" | "Stabilized — observed in the ED with monitoring" |
| "Wrong answer" | "Critical action missed" / "Correct but delayed" |
| "Oops, something broke" | "The simulation could not be started." |
| "Learn medicine faster!" | "Practice clinical decisions on dynamic patient cases." |

**Rules**

- Outcomes are stated as clinical facts, not judgments of the learner.
- Timing language is exact: "on time (02:05)", "correct but delayed (06:10)".
- Safety penalties and efficiency penalties are never described with the same
  word ("harmful" vs "unnecessary").
- No unsupported claims (exam results, speed of learning, patient outcomes).
- Governance honesty is part of the voice: demo builds say clinical validation
  is pending; the disclaimer is readable, not buried.
- No exclamation marks in clinical contexts. No emoji in clinical contexts.
- Buttons are verbs: "Enter simulation", "Replay this case", "Reset local
  progress".
- Errors: one plain sentence + a way forward (Retry / Back). Never stack
  traces, codes-first messages, or bridge jargon.
